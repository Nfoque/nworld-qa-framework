## Brand Usage

### Brand Vision

The N is the **common element across all brands** and the distinctive sign of the company. It is the primary resource for representing identity and should be used by default in most external communications: presentations, proposals, commercial materials, and social media posts.

### Operational Vision

Specific group brands are used only when they add value:

- **Specialization** — In sectors where the brand is recognized (e.g., Nfq in Banking, Insurance and Real Estate; Nter in tech environments; Nforce in recruiting)
- **Local presence** — When there is a regional link (e.g., Nter in Jaen, NWorld in Zaragoza)
- **Group & new initiatives** — NWorld is applied as the umbrella for the conglomerate and strategic projects

### Brand Claim

Every brand has a claim that always accompanies the logo. It provides context about the sector or specialization and must always be included when using the full brand mark.

### Usage Rules

**DO:**
- Use the N as the default corporate symbol
- Accompany the full logo with its corresponding claim
- Apply specific brands only when they reinforce specialization or local context

**DON'T:**
- Add or remove elements from the logo or claim
- Use specific brands without strategic justification
- Omit the claim in communications with the full logo

### Neutral Colors

| Name | HEX |
|------|-----|
| **Black** | `#100D25` |
| **Grey 700** | `#5D6066` |
| **Grey 500** | `#82858D` |
| **Grey 300** | `#BFBFBF` |
| **Grey 200** | `#E1E1E1` |
| **Grey 100** | `#F3F3F3` |

### Color Usage Rules

**DO:**
- Prioritize a **single primary color as the main accent** in key pieces
- Always ensure **sufficient contrast** to maintain content legibility
- Use colors **selectively and strategically**, avoiding saturation
- Use neutral tones as the **predominant base** for backgrounds and typography
- Apply different **greys to mark hierarchies**
- Combine with primary colors for **balanced contrast**

**DON'T:**
- Alter HEX values or create unapproved variations
- Use combinations that reduce legibility (e.g., red text on orange background)
- Use neutral colors as substitutes for primaries in strategic pieces
- Overuse very dark tones

## Typography

### Primary: Sora

Modern, clear, and versatile style. The foundation of brand communication.

- Used for **titles, headings, and highlighted text**
- Conveys clarity, innovation, and personality
- Available via Google Fonts

### Secondary: Instrument Sans

Complements Sora as the secondary typeface. Neutral, legible, and balances text.

- Used for **body text, subtitles, and supporting content**
- Ensures legibility in long paragraphs and digital formats
- Available via Google Fonts

## Photography

Photography is a key pillar of the brand's visual identity. It defines the tone of communication and conveys professionalism, approachability, and innovation.

### Photography Style

- Clear, natural, and professional
- Themes: innovation, technology, teamwork, positive impact
- No excessive editing or filters

### Usage Rules

**DO:**
- Use brand-approved photographs when possible
- Aim for a **clear, natural, and professional style**, avoiding excessive editing
- Use images that reinforce brand values: **innovation, tech, teamwork, positive impact**

**DON'T:**
- Alter images with filters or effects
- Mix very different styles in the same material
- Include **low-quality images**, pixelated or with watermarks
